Welcome

One school. Connected workspaces.

Afaq brings school records and daily work into one system. Each person signs in to a workspace shaped around their responsibilities.

Who uses Afaq?
  • Administrator: oversees school setup, records and approvals.
  • Finance Officer: follows invoices, payments, fees and expenses.
  • Teacher: manages assigned classes and records learning.
  • Parent: follows linked children and school communication.
  • Learner: accesses courses and personal learning records.
How to use this handbook

Start with the shared navigation guide, then follow the chapter for your role. The screen labels in bold match the app.

What you can see depends on your account, school setup and assigned permissions. Some pages may be empty until the school publishes or records information.

PROPOSED GOVERNANCE
Target operating model · partly implemented

Three responsibilities. Clear boundaries.

Keep platform operation, school administration and security operations separate. A role should receive only the access needed for its duties.

PLATFORM

Super Admin

Governs the Afaq service across madrassas: tenant onboarding, platform-wide configuration, service plans, platform-level incidents and aggregate service health.

SCHOOL TENANT

Madrassa Admin

Governs one madrassa's people and operations: local users, classes, assignments, school records, approvals and school-level policies.

COMPANY OPERATIONS

IT / Security Operator

Protects and operates the technical service: logs, monitoring, alerts, vulnerabilities, backups, incident response and security evidence.

Target authority and limits

Super Admin

  • Create, configure, suspend or restore madrassa tenants under approved policy.
  • Manage platform-wide settings, service status and escalation.
  • View aggregate tenant/service health; tenant-content access is not part of routine oversight.
  • Approve exceptional cross-tenant support access only when necessary, scoped, time-limited and audited.

Madrassa Admin

  • Manage users and records inside their own madrassa only.
  • Assign staff roles and class/branch scope using least privilege.
  • Review and publish school records; manage local settings and respond to school requests.
  • Cannot create other tenants, change platform security settings or inspect another madrassa.

IT / Security Operator

  • Operate infrastructure, monitoring, access controls, backups and security tooling.
  • Review technical metadata and security logs for service protection.
  • Cannot approve school reports, change grades, post payments or administer school records.
  • Production data access requires an incident/ticket, approval, minimum scope, expiry and audit trail.

Existing school roles remain

Accountant, Teacher, Parent and Learner keep their current tenant-scoped duties. Platform or IT titles must not silently inherit these business permissions.

A Super Admin is not an all-purpose school user; IT is not a substitute school administrator.

The cards describe the target model, not a list of live permissions. See page 20 for implemented capabilities and deployment limits.

Who creates a madrassa?

Approve first. Provision second. Activate last.

Current foundation: independent approval, IT creation of an inactive madrassa, and a read-only progress view. Invitation acceptance and activation logic are tested internally; email delivery is not connected.

›

›

›
First Admin accepts invite
›
Tenant activated

1 · Request and approve · Console

  • Super Admin records the school name, unique code and nominated admin email.
  • A different Super Admin reviews and approves or rejects with a reason.
  • Approval does not create a school or grant access. Verify ownership outside the form.

2 · Create inactive school · Console

  • IT opens Madrassa setup, then Review setup.
  • After checks pass, confirm Create inactive madrassa. Super Admin cannot execute this IT action.
  • Status stays PENDING_ACTIVATION. A retry of the same application does not create a duplicate.

3 · First Admin · Internal foundation

  • A private, one-use invitation expires after 24 hours; only its hash is stored.
  • Acceptance creates a DISABLED school Admin, not a login session.
  • The /school-invitation page is for testing. The acceptance API remains disabled; no email is sent.

4 · Activation · Internal foundation

  • A Super Admin other than the requester checks the matching acceptance receipt.
  • Record evidence and attest to isolation, access controls, audit, recovery and support checks.
  • School and Admin activate together with audit. These are operator attestations, not automatic verification; no activation UI is connected.

Read the current onboarding status

ONBOARDING PROGRESS

Super Admin and IT can inspect completed milestones and the next pending stage.

INVITATION EXPIRED

A replacement is needed through the approved delivery workflow, not a shared token.

REVIEW REQUIRED

Investigate inconsistent or suspended access; do not bypass the checks.

Approval is not activation. An invitation record is not proof of email delivery. If creation times out, refresh the queue before retrying the same application. No live deployment or real invitation sending is implied by this handbook.

PROPOSED GOVERNANCE
Company IT / Security Operator

Protect the service. Prove what happened.

The IT function owns technical safeguards and response, not the madrassas' academic or financial decisions.

1 · Health and reliability

  • Monitor API, database, queue, storage, media and payment integration health.
  • Track availability, latency, error rates, capacity and backup completion.
  • Test restore procedures; report outages and recovery status to stakeholders.

2 · Logs and audit

  • Centralise authentication, privilege changes, admin actions, finance events and security alerts.
  • Protect logs from alteration; restrict access and define retention and deletion schedules.
  • Do not log passwords, tokens, payment secrets or unnecessary student/parent content.

3 · Threats and vulnerabilities

  • Triage suspicious sign-ins, abuse, malware indicators and unusual cross-tenant access.
  • Track dependencies, patches, secrets rotation and remediation deadlines.
  • Escalate confirmed or suspected incidents through a documented severity process.

4 · Identity and change control

  • Require strong authentication for privileged accounts and review access regularly.
  • Use named accounts; prohibit shared Super Admin or operator credentials.
  • Record production changes, approvals, rollback plans and maintenance windows.

Incident response and delivery plan

Detect and preserve evidence
›
Triage and contain
›
Notify owners and investigate
›
Recover and review
PHASE 1 · GOVERN

Name role owners, tenant boundaries, approval rules, incident severity and data-retention policy.

PHASE 2 · ENFORCE

Add platform roles, scoped permissions, MFA, immutable audit events and tested tenant-isolation checks.

PHASE 3 · OPERATE

Connect monitoring and alerts, rehearse incident/restore workflows, review metrics and improve controls.

Current boundary: the console reads platform audit events and database/Redis health. Threat detection, backup verification, incident workflows and emergency tenant access are not connected. Tested source code is not evidence of a production deployment; see page 20.

FINANCE OFFICER
02

Maintain fees and finance records

Finance Officer · Finance tools

Fee structures

Search the rate register, review active or inactive structures, and open a form to record a fee amount and billing cycle. Check the branch or school scope before saving.

Expenses

Record a positive amount, expense date and optional description. Use date filters and record details to review entries already in the register.

Financial reports

Select a year and review reported totals and monthly collections and expenses. Preview or save the available CSV export for further analysis.

Finance inbox

Review finance inquiries, search and filter by status, then update a request to NEW, CONTACTED or CLOSED as appropriate.

Before recording an expense

  1. Check that the expense has not already been entered.
  2. Confirm the amount, date and currency shown for the record.
  3. Add a useful description when it helps identify the purpose.
  4. Save once, then confirm the entry appears in the register.

A fee structure is a rate record. Saving it does not automatically create invoices. An expense record documents a cost; it does not transfer money from a bank.

Read financial totals carefully

  • Summary totals reflect what the API reports for the selected period.
  • Invoiced minus collected is a period difference, not necessarily today's debtor balance.
  • Individual entries display their recorded currency. Do not add mixed currencies without conversion.
  • CSV exports contain financial information. Choose an appropriate folder and recipient.

Maintain an audit trail. Use the official register and school procedures to investigate discrepancies. Do not treat an inquiry status as proof that a message was sent or a payment was received.

TROUBLESHOOTING
When something does not look right

Check the record before you retry.

A quick, safe sequence for the issues users most often encounter. Your role determines which records and actions are available.

Cannot sign in

  1. Check the school-issued email or phone number and remove accidental spaces.
  2. Confirm your internet or configured school network is working.
  3. Retry once. If it still fails, ask the school office to check the account.

A child, class or record is missing

  1. Check the selected child, class, date, period and filters.
  2. Refresh the page once and confirm the connection.
  3. Ask the school to verify the family link, class assignment or publication status.

Payment is pending or unclear

  1. Open the invoice and inspect its linked payment status and reference.
  2. Do not submit another payment just because the first screen timed out.
  3. Share the invoice or transaction reference with the finance office for verification.

Course or lesson is locked

  1. Read the access label: available, preview, access required or locked.
  2. For paid content, check the related request and invoice status.
  3. Ask the school office to review access if payment or approval is already confirmed.

Video, audio or document will not open

  1. Check your connection and try the lesson resource again.
  2. Confirm the selected lesson has published content and that your access allows it.
  3. Send support the course, lesson and resource name; never send your password.

My change or completion is not showing

  1. Look for a saved or submitted confirmation before leaving the page.
  2. Refresh and inspect the record status before entering it again.
  3. For reports or assessments, check whether administrator review is still pending.

When contacting support: include your role, page name, approximate time and a safe record reference. Do not send passwords, full payment card details or another user's private information.

QUICK REFERENCE
Shared language

Key terms at a glance.

These terms help staff, families and learners understand what a record represents and what its status means.

Learning content

Subject is a learning area, such as Qur'an or Mathematics. A course organises online learning for a subject. A lesson is an individual study unit inside a course.

Finance records

A fee structure defines a rate. An invoice records an amount due. A payment records an attempt or collection, depending on its status. A receipt is evidence available for a completed payment.

Work stages

Draft is being prepared. Submitted is waiting for review. Under review means a reviewer is checking it. Published is released to the intended audience.

Access and progress

Available can be opened. Preview is a limited sample. Access required needs payment or approval. Locked is not currently open to the learner.

Keep progress types separate. Course lesson completion, teacher-recorded assessment results, attendance and Qur'an learning records are different measures and may update at different times.

Use the record's current status. A term may be displayed differently by a school or workflow. When in doubt, open the record details or ask the responsible office.

PLATFORM OPERATIONS
Implementation snapshot · 8 October 2026

A separate workspace for company operations.

Super Admin and IT Security use platform identities. Madrassa Admin, Accountant, Teacher, Parent and Learner remain school identities; school credentials do not grant platform access.

Built and tested, not deployed by this update. Ask the deployment administrator for the configured platform address. The app route is /platform (commonly /#/platform on web). No default platform password or public account registration is provided.

1 · Sign in securely

Use your named company account, password and six-digit authenticator code. The session lasts 15 minutes; sign in again when it expires. Never share passwords, setup keys or authenticator codes.

2 · Check your workspace

Super Admin: overview, applications, company accounts, audit, health and own sessions.

IT Security: Madrassa setup, approved onboarding progress, audit, health and own sessions. No school business permissions.

3 · Review madrassa applications

A Super Admin records the request. A different Super Admin approves or rejects it with a reason. A reviewed decision cannot be overwritten. Approval alone creates no school and sends no invitation.

4 · Inspect audit and health

Audit trail: filter by event, role and period, then open event details.

System health: inspect database and Redis availability. An unavailable response is not proof that services are healthy.

My sessions: close access you no longer need

  1. Open My sessions, select your current or another session, and confirm revocation.
  2. Sign out all sessions requires confirmation and affects your company account only.
  3. If server sign-out cannot be confirmed, local sign-out still completes after the attempt. Other sessions may remain active; sign in and review them.

Suspend deliberately. Restore independently.

Find the right account

Open Company accounts. Filter by Account role and Account status, or choose Clear account filters. Verify name, email and account ID before acting. A filter change returns to page one.

Review the evidence

Open Access history to read who disabled or restored access, when and why. Use Older / Newer for groups of 20 records and Retry after a failed request. Times are UTC; reasons are operator statements, not independently verified findings.

Disable IT access

  1. Select an active IT account.
  2. Choose Disable IT account.
  3. Enter a 10–500 character reason, without secrets.
  4. Choose Confirm disable.

Login is blocked and all its sessions are revoked. Status, revocation and audit commit together; an audit failure rolls the change back.

Restore IT access

  1. A different Super Admin reviews the suspension and its resolution.
  2. Choose Review restoration.
  3. Record the review reason and choose Confirm restore.

The account must have enrolled MFA and a recorded suspension. Old sessions stay revoked; IT signs in again with password and MFA.

Protected boundaries. You cannot disable yourself or a Super Admin here. The suspending administrator cannot approve restoration. IT cannot list company accounts or read reason-bearing access history. Shared audit views omit these reasons.

When an action does not complete

Refresh and check the latest status before retrying. A changed record may reject the request; a denied restoration requires another authorised reviewer. Never treat a connection failure as confirmation of success.

History pages may shift when new events arrive; this is not a fixed forensic export. The history window closes at session expiry. Requests already in flight may finish after access is disabled. Do not use restoration as a substitute for investigating a suspected compromise.
